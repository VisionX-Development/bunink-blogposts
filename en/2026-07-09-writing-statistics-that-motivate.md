Writing is often a quiet craft. You sit with a text, change a sentence, cut a paragraph, keep going — and by the end of the day it's hard to say whether you actually made progress. This is exactly where **Writing statistics** in bun.ink comes in: it makes visible what usually stays hidden while you write. How much you've written. For how long. And how your project grows over weeks and months.

One important note up front: the statistics count **you**, not your text. What's recorded are metrics like word counts and writing time — not the content of your documents. The numbers belong to your account and are private.

## Where to find the statistics

In the writer at `/writer` there's a dedicated button in the toolbar for **Writing statistics**. One click opens a window with three tabs:

- **Document** – figures for the text you currently have open.
- **Project** – the overview of an entire writing project.
- **Overview** – your long-term writing activity and motivation.

So depending on the situation, you can zoom in — on a single text — or zoom out — on your writing as a whole.

## Document: a look at the current text

The **Document** tab always refers to the document you currently have open and active. It shows you at a glance:

- **Words** – the word count of the current text.
- **Characters** – the number of characters.
- **Paragraphs** – how many paragraphs the text has.
- **Reading time** – an estimate of how long it takes to read (calculated at around 200 words per minute).

For documents that have already been saved, two more values are added:

- **Saved versions** – how many snapshots of this document exist.
- **Change volume** – how many characters have changed across those versions.

This is handy when you're working towards a particular length — a short story, a newspaper article with a character limit, or a chapter with a target word count. Instead of guessing, you can see exactly where you stand.

## Project: the bigger picture

A single document is rarely the whole project. A novel consists of chapters, a feature of research notes and drafts, a non-fiction book of many building blocks. The **Project** tab brings all of that together:

- **Total words** – the word count across all documents in the project.
- **Documents** – how many documents the project contains.
- **Most words** – which documents are the longest.
- **By folder** – how the words are distributed across your folder structure.

You can also set a **project goal** in words. A progress bar then shows how close you already are to your overall target — the 80,000 words of a novel manuscript, for example.

If your project is connected to **GitHub**, a section on **GitHub activity** also appears: the commits of the last 120 days, the commits from this week, and the most recent entries. That way you can see your version history right alongside your writing.

## Overview: motivation over time

The **Overview** tab is the heart of the matter for anyone who wants to keep at it. It gathers your writing activity over a long period and turns it into something that motivates rather than merely informs.

At the very top is your **daily goal**. You decide for yourself how many words you want to write per day (the default is 500). A progress bar shows how far you've come today.

Below that you'll find your key figures as tiles:

- **Words today** and the totals for **this week**, **this month** and **all time**.
- **Streak** – how many days in a row you've hit your daily goal, along with your longest streak.
- **Writing time today** – how long you've actively been writing today.
- **Net words** – words written minus words deleted.
- **Average per active day** and **words per hour**.

The **Streak** is deliberately designed to be encouraging: as long as you reach your daily goal, it grows day by day. And if you haven't reached your goal yet today, the streak does **not** break — the day isn't over, after all. That takes the pressure off and rewards consistency rather than perfection.

Then there's the **next milestone**: 10,000, 25,000, 50,000, 100,000 words and onwards. A bar shows how close you are to the next round number — a small but surprisingly effective bit of encouragement.

### The heatmap: a year of writing at a glance

At the bottom of the overview sits the **activity heatmap** for the past twelve months. Each day is a small square; the more you wrote on a given day, the stronger its colour. If you know GitHub, you'll recognise the pattern immediately.

This view tells a story that individual numbers can't: the story of your writing habit. You can see the productive weeks, the breaks, the slow restarts. Hover over a square and it shows you the date and word count for that day.

## How the numbers come about

The statistics run quietly in the background while you write. A few points to help you interpret the values:

- At short intervals the app compares the word count of your text and infers from that how many words have been added or removed. This is a deliberate **estimate**, not an exact comparison of every single change.
- **Writing time** only counts when you're genuinely active. Longer pauses are recognised as breaks and aren't included.
- Everything is recorded by **local calendar day** — "today" means your today.
- The statistics count **regardless of branch**. Whether you're writing on the main version or on a GitHub branch: your activity is recorded without changing the rules for how your texts are saved.
- Your deltas are cached, so nothing is lost even if your connection drops briefly.

## How this helps you write

Numbers alone don't write a book. But they can support habits, and habits write books.

- **Set realistic goals.** A daily word target makes a big project tangible. Not "write a novel", but "500 words today".
- **See yourself keeping at it.** The streak and heatmap reward consistency. It's easier not to break a chain you can actually see.
- **Keep the overview.** On long projects, your sense of scale is quickly lost. The project figures show you where the weight lies and where there's still little to show.
- **Get to know yourself better.** Words per hour and average per day gradually reveal how your writing actually works — and when you're most productive.

The Writing statistics in bun.ink aren't a stern supervisor. They're more like a quiet companion that shows you, every now and then, how far you've already come. Sometimes that's exactly the little nudge that turns a text you've started into a project you finish.
